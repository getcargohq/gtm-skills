# Ideal customer profile

PLACEHOLDER. This is a worked example for a company selling GTM automation to
technical revenue teams. Replace every line with yours before deploying, and
keep it in the workspace context repo rather than in a system prompt: this file
is what the tiering agent reads, and editing it changes the next tier without a
deploy.

## Who buys

B2B software, developer infrastructure, and security companies that already run
a technical go-to-market motion, or are visibly staffing one. The buying signal
is a person: a GTM engineer, a technical RevOps lead, or a growth engineer with
a mandate to automate rather than to report.

## Firmographics

- Industry: software development, IT services and consulting, internet and
  technology, computer and network security
- Headcount: 20 to 500
- Ownership: privately held or public. Not a subsidiary being wound down, not
  an agency reselling someone else's stack
- Geography: no restriction, but the buying committee must operate in English

## Disqualifiers

Any one of these ends the evaluation. They are not "medium fit".

- Above 500 employees. The 500 to 5,000 band is out of scope, not an exception
- Consumer, marketplace, or services-only businesses with no software product
- Agencies and consultancies with no in-house automation practice
- No technical champion anywhere in the revenue org, and no evidence of hiring
  one
- Already committed to a competing platform on a multi-year contract

## What "already running the motion" looks like

Evidence that counts, in the order it is worth trusting:

1. An open role, or a filled one, whose title names GTM engineering, revenue
   operations engineering, or growth engineering
2. Public writing (engineering blog, conference talk, changelog) about
   automating pipeline, enrichment, or routing
3. A public stack that already includes a warehouse, a reverse-ETL tool, or a
   CRM with custom objects
4. Headcount growth in revenue roles outpacing headcount growth overall
